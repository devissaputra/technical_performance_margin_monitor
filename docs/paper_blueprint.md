# Paper Blueprint

## Working title
**Forecast Maturity as a Technical Performance Measure: Fixed-Checkpoint Evidence from NASA Battery Aging Trajectories**

## Motivation
Systems engineering uses technical performance measures to identify emerging risk before requirements are missed. A forecast of future margin exhaustion is useful only if its own stability is visible to decision makers.

## Research gap
A single threshold-crossing forecast can look precise while changing sharply as more evidence arrives. The engineering-management problem is therefore two-dimensional: current margin and forecast maturity.

## Method
Use NASA cells B0005, B0006, B0007, and B0018. At cycles 40, 60, and 80, fit a transparent linear capacity trend using only data available at that checkpoint and project the NASA 1.4 Ah EOL threshold.

## Results
Crossing-cycle MAE across cells with observed crossings decreases from 102.399 to 35.852 to 11.942 cycles. Mean projection revision declines from 100.046 cycles at the 60-cycle update to 38.012 at the 80-cycle update. B0007 produces a false early warning at checkpoint 80.

## Contribution
The bundle demonstrates why forecast stability should accompany the forecast itself in technical-performance monitoring.

## Limitations
Four cells, one transparent model family, one NASA EOL threshold, no uncertainty calibration, no cross-cell training, and no claim of production readiness.
